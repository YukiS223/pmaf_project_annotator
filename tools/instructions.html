PMAF Annotator — Instructions

Local tool for labeling auroral forms in all-sky video with per-frame masks and stable track IDs. Everything runs offline on this computer — nothing is uploaded.

1 Start the tool

  • macOS (Apple silicon): first launch — right-click START_HERE.command → Open → Open. Afterwards a normal double-click works.
  • Windows: double-click START_HERE.exe. If SmartScreen objects: More info → Run anyway.
  • Your browser opens http://127.0.0.1:8798/ (go there manually if not). Use Chrome or Edge. Keep the terminal/console window open — closing it stops the tool.
  • Run only one copy at a time; a second launch refuses to start.

2 Create a project from a video

On the home screen, drop an AVI/MP4 onto the upload box, or click Prepare next to a video the tool found beside its folder. Preparation extracts every frame and reads the burned-in UT clock (2–6 minutes and ~2 GB of disk per 1-hour clip). When it says ready, click Annotate.

3 The annotation loop

  1. N — create a track. One track = one physical form; its ID follows it across frames.
  2. Circle the form: hold the mouse button and trace loosely around it; release to close the outline. Drag inside the outline to move it; drawing again (starting outside it) replaces it.
  3. S — Snap: fits a pixel mask to the bright aurora inside your outline (never grows beyond it). Move the threshold slider — the mask updates live; fix details with the brush (B add, X erase).
  4. A — Accept & advance: stores the mask, moves to the next frame, copies the outline forward and shows a preview snap (dashed outline, softer mask). Previews are never saved — adjust, then A again. Z discards a preview or an unfinished edit.
  5. V — the form is not visible on this frame (absent).
  6. Q — whole frame unusable (clouds, glare, corruption); press again to undo.
  • Sky views: D toggles Original / Enhanced. Enhanced flattens twilight/sunlight gradients and preserves faint aurora — label in whichever view shows the form best. Views only change what you see; masks always snap to the underlying image data.
  • Editing an accepted frame (moving its outline or brushing) opens a preview copy; the stored mask is untouched until you press A to re-accept.
  • A track created by mistake: Delete track… in the right panel.
  • Press ? in the app for the full keyboard map; H hides masks for a blink test.

4 Labeling rules

  • Do not label: the quasi-static background oval/arc a form detaches from, airglow patches, moonlit clouds, city glow, or forms that brighten but never move.
  • Mask boundary: cover pixels visibly brighter than the local background that belong to the form — the clearly-emitting region, not the faintest halo. Consistency beats single-frame perfection: use similar thresholds for similar forms and keep the propagated preview's threshold unless it is visibly wrong.
  • Faint forms: judge in the Enhanced view; blink against neighbouring frames (arrow keys) — real forms move coherently, noise does not.
  • Forms crossing the sky-circle edge: label what is inside (clipped automatically).
  • Track life: start at the first frame where the form is distinctly identifiable; use Starts here to mark all earlier frames absent in one click. After its last frame, press Ends here. Both are undoable from the events list — undoing Ends here lets the track continue.
  • Reappearance: same track if position and motion are clearly continuous and the gap is under ~30 s; otherwise a new track.
  • Split: the part continuing the original motion keeps the track; create a new track for the other part. Merge: keep the larger/brighter track and press Ends here on the other. Masks never overlap — give contested pixels to the form they most plausibly belong to.
  • Density: annotate every frame during onset, rapid motion, splits, merges and disappearance. In stable spans you may set the advance step to 2, but skipped frames stay unreviewed and are not exported — fill them in if dense labels are needed.
  • Timing: frames whose HUD shows [interp] or [nominal] have reconstructed clock times — annotate normally, but report clips with many of them.
  • When a rule is ambiguous, ask the PI rather than guessing.

5 Saving and quality

  • Everything autosaves continuously (indicator in the top bar) — no export needed to keep your work. Relaunching the tool reopens your project on the exact frame, track and view you left.
  • All work lives in projects/ — back that folder up. Time-bucketed safety copies are kept in projects/<name>/backups/; after a crash, copy the newest over annotations.json.

Checklist before export:

  • Timeline lanes: no dark (unreviewed) gaps inside each track's life.
  • Blink test (H) on a few random accepted frames: the mask hugs the form.
  • No track with 0 accepted frames; Ends here recorded where tracks die or merge.

6 Exporting labels (for the training pipeline)

Click Export… in the toolbar. Export is fail-closed: it validates everything and writes either a complete dataset or a .FAILED folder with the report. Output in projects/<name>/export/<timestamp>/:

filecontents
train_cocovis.jsonCOCO-VIS (YouTube-VIS style): per-track per-frame RLE masks, boxes, areas; original frame indices and per-frame UT times
masks/%06d.pnguint16 instance-ID maps (0 = background, value = track id)
lut.jsonpixel value → track uuid/name/color
audit.json, report.json, checksums.txt full annotation record, validator results, sha256 sums
  • null segmentation = reviewed and absent (a true negative). Frames missing from the export were not reviewed — never treat them as negatives.
  • file_names resolve against videos[].image_root (the project folder; frame PNGs are not copied into the export).
  • Group train/val splits by videos[].night_id — clips from the same night are correlated and must stay in one split.
  • True frame cadence is ~1.0145 s and drifts — use the exported UT timestamps, never frame index × 1 s.
